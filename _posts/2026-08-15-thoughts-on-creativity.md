# How Creativity becomes Technical and How AI Breaks the Flow

I think that everything creative takes on a more technical character when done enough times. Think about drawing. At first, you would probably think about what you're going to make, how you should approach it, and what scene the object is in. But when you draw the same image, at a certain point it's going to become more of a "How much pressure should I put on this stroke?" or "How should the clothing wrap around the character?" type of thing. Though obviously, drawing the same image over and over again is neither fun nor valued.<br>
<small>im not an artist so maybe i'm just a larp lmao</small>

This is not me saying that this is a bad thing in any way, quite the opposite actually. A more practical example of this would be in coding. When you first start, you'll think about the projects you'll do, what tech stack to utilize, and laying out the frame for your project. And in the end it'll become an optimization grind. Debugging, lowering RAM usage, cleaning up code, and writing documentation. The things some would call "mundane" or "boring". Though unlike drawing the same image over and over, this is valued in the tech space. Mainly because the tech space is well... more *technical* than the art space and requires you to have the most efficient way to do something.

Here's the part where I talk about AI, specifically LLMs. When you start learning a new skill or start a new project, using AI can actually hinder you. The part where you have to think on how to approach it or experiment with different things regarding the field? Gone. You've let AI do that part for you. This is dangerous when you have to actually understand what you're doing. The employer isn't going to let you use AI in the interview. **LEARN WHAT YOU ARE DOING.** Read, read, and read again. This is the most crucial skill in your arsenal. Do not for even once think that you can avoid learning by using AI. No, that is not learning. That's copying. You could have AI assist you in learning such as using it to make dummy questions, make tests for you, and such. But much like regular studying, that requires willpower. That's just how learning is.

In an era where most Gen Z and Gen Alpha students are using AI for their assignments, you have to stand out. You cannot delegate your work to AI. As the famous philosopher, dsyncronized once said: "You ought not use AI on your projects, my child! Learn! Grow! And something along those lines." But alas, his words were not fully realized by the younger generation. Such a tragedy. It is to my utmost sorrow that he was unable to single-handedly shape a generation or two. That aside, the rise of AI reliance is definitely not of unworthy concern.

There is also a benefit to using AI in the tech field. Let's think back to when I talked about the technical side of coding. AI is objectively better than you at coding as a whole. That's just the fact. Optimizing code or writing documentation aren't relatively low-level tasks to do, they usually take a long time despite offering a modest value. That's where AI comes along and does those things. It is not hands-free. You will still need to verify every line of code and every piece of information that the AI has written. But it can substantially reduce the time required to complete those tasks. Like any tool, it has its limitations and benefits. And like any tool, you cannot replace your brain with it.
